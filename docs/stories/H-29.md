# H-29 — Revocation is reversible by the recipient
## Story
- A recipient who keeps the share their browser received can post it back after the sender ends
  access, and the link opens again. **Revocation is undone by the person it is revoking.**

## Context
From the adversarial review (review-1, gpt-5.6-sol, 2026-09-12), rated critical at confidence 99,
and verified independently before filing.

`app/api/holder/share/route.ts` has **no authentication of any kind**. `putShare` uses `SET NX`, so
it refuses only while a key exists. `deleteShare` frees the slot. The recipient legitimately holds
the share — `/api/holder/unlock` returns it to them, by design — so after a revoke they can re-post
it with the public commitment and restore their own access until the Arkiv grant expires.

The reviewer's probe called the real functions and returned
`{"firstStore":true,"afterRevokeStore":true}`.

This was not introduced by H-11. The share route was written before revoke existed, when nothing
could delete a key, so write-once was sufficient. Adding revoke turned an empty slot into a
reachable state and nobody revisited the route.

**A second, related defect from the same review (critical, confidence 99).** Share read and
deletion are not serialized: an unlock that has already read the share returns it even though a
revoke completed in between. The reviewer paused the real `resolveUnlock` after its read, ran the
real `performRevoke` to success, and the paused unlock still returned the share.

## What to build
- **Authenticate the share route.** Only the grant's sender may store a share for an entity key.
  `lib/revoke.ts` already has the scheme — a domain-prefixed signature over the entity key and a
  timestamp, recovered and compared against the grant's `sender`. Use a distinct action string, as
  `revoke` and `access-log` already do, so one signature is not the other.
- **Make revocation permanent.** An empty slot must not be refillable while the grant is live. A
  tombstone under the same TTL as the grant, checked by `putShare`, is the straightforward shape.
  `createSend` writes the grant before posting the share, so a legitimate first write is
  distinguishable from a refill.
- **Close the race.** Re-check that the share still exists after the read and before returning it,
  or restructure so the read is the thing that can fail. The product claim — *"She lost access the
  moment you tapped it"* — should be true for an unlock in flight, or the copy must change.

## Do not
Do not fix this by removing the share route. The sender needs it, and `createSend` throws if the
handoff fails, which is the correct failure.
