# H-30 — The README opens by describing the architecture it replaced
## Story
- The README's first three sections describe v1 — the grant carrying half the key, and no server of
  ours in the read path. Its middle sections describe the split-key design that actually ships. The
  document contradicts itself, and the opening is what people read.

## Context
From the adversarial review, rated critical at confidence 100, verified before filing.

`README.md:34` — *"half is written into an Arkiv grant that carries an expiry"*. False since the
rewrite: a v2 grant carries a Swarm reference and a SHA-256 commitment, and **no key material**,
which was the entire point.

`README.md:54` — *"There is no server of ours in the read path at all, and so nothing we could be
compelled to keep serving."* Also false. The holder is ours, it is in the read path, and it can be
compelled — the README's own *"The cost, stated plainly"* says so. The reviewer also flags
`README.md:109`, `:487` (*"exactly one server route"*, against four holder routes now) and `:559`.

**And the adoption section over-claims** (critical, confidence 100). `README.md:72` says a short
consult leaves *"nothing left over afterward"* and `:79` says *"nothing left behind when it
expires"* — both contradicted by `README.md:202-204`, the repository's own *"What we never
claimed"*: a recipient who screenshots during the window keeps the screenshot.

That section was reviewed at merge specifically for not contradicting the README, and this was
missed. Worth recording, because it is the same failure the README exists to document.

## What to build
Bring the opening in line with what ships, and keep the correction visible rather than quietly
rewriting history — the retraction is the most credible thing in this repository. The middle
sections are already right; this is the front matter catching up to them.

Then re-read every superlative in the adoption section against *"What we never claimed"*.
